# QuantaTale global audiovisual production standards

These standards apply to **every topic, format, renderer, storyboard and publishing workflow**. They are not optional preferences attached to one sample video.

## 1. Narration and sound
- Kokoro-82M is the narration engine; `af_heart` is the default voice.
- eSpeak NG may be installed only as a Kokoro phonemization dependency. It must never silently become the spoken voice.
- Audio must be checked for clipping, silence, abrupt cuts, pronunciation and synchronization before publication.
- Narration pace and scene timing must allow the audience to understand each idea.

## 2. Holographic visual identity
- The subject, environment, diagrams and explanatory objects must themselves look holographic—not merely labels placed over ordinary flat art.
- Use deep navy/black environments, cyan/electric-blue and violet luminous outlines, transparent layered surfaces, depth, volumetric light, particles, scanlines and moving light trails.
- Use camera motion, object motion, transformations and evolving scientific processes. A static image with animated captions is not sufficient.
- Do not use stock footage or generic decorative visuals as substitutes for explanatory animation.

## 3. Narration-to-visual correspondence
- Every important factual concept in the narration must have a corresponding visible scene or diagram.
- Scene plans must explain the mechanism, sequence, scale, comparison or causal relationship being narrated.
- Visuals must remain understandable with the audio muted; captions and labels must be readable and synchronized.
- Do not imply a scientifically false mechanism for visual convenience. For example, the sky-blue storyboard shows the colours already present in white sunlight and wavelength-dependent scattering; it must not depict the atmosphere as a prism.

## 4. Motion and editorial quality gates
- Confirm actual frame-to-frame movement in subjects, particles, light, camera or diagram states.
- Confirm scene transitions follow the narration's logic.
- Confirm captions do not obscure key visuals, fit the safe area and remain legible on a phone.
- Confirm voice, visuals, narration and source-backed claims agree.
- Any failed gate blocks publication. Automated checks support but do not replace human review.

## 5. Reusable production architecture
Every new video specification must include a topic-specific storyboard mapping narration beats to:
1. The concept being explained.
2. The visible subject or mechanism.
3. Its holographic treatment.
4. Its movement or transformation.
5. The narration/caption timing.
6. The evidence-backed fact it illustrates.

A topic must not be rendered using another topic's storyboard. A sky-blue scene sequence cannot be reused for a sunset, wildlife, history, engineering or other unrelated explanation. The renderer must either support the requested storyboard profile or fail clearly; it must never silently produce mismatched visuals.

## 6. Review history carried forward
The standards above incorporate the user's review of the sky-blue sample:
- the first render was too static;
- the initial narration sounded robotic, leading to the switch to Kokoro;
- all colours and the eye needed to be visible when mentioned;
- the entire visual world, not only its labels, must be holographic;
- sunlight, atmospheric scattering and the path to the viewer's eye must be shown clearly.

Future reviews should update this shared standard and its automated acceptance tests, not just patch an individual video.
